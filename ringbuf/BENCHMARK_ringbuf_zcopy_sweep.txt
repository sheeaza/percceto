ringbuf_zcopy+mu producer sweep -- results and analysis
=======================================================

Benchmark:  bench_ringbuf_zcopy_sweep.c   (target: bench_ringbuf_zcopy_sweep)
Build:      cshell dev shell, clang 21.1.8, Ninja, -O2 -g
Host:       20 CPUs online; consumer pinned to CPU 0, producers to CPUs 1..19
            (cycling, so 20 and 24 producers share cores)
Geometry:   slot = 40 B, capacity = 2^18 (262144), fixed
Runs:       2,000,000 messages per run, median-throughput run of 5 reported
Modes:      lossless and overwrite (drop-oldest)
Variant:    ringbuf_zcopy with the caller's pthread mutex around reserve only.
            The 1-producer row takes no lock; the "1+lock" row takes it.

Analysis
--------

1. One producer is the fast path.
   With one producer there is no caller lock and no contention. Each message
   costs about 9.4 ns of wall time (106.69 Mm/s) and 18.5 ns of CPU. The
   consumer almost never waits (9.6 pop retries per 1000).

2. The lock alone costs about 2.7x at one producer.
   The "1+lock" row takes the same single producer and wraps reserve in the
   mutex. Throughput falls from 106.69 to 40.22 Mm/s, about 15 ns extra per
   message. This is the uncontended lock/unlock pair in the producer's path,
   and it also starves the consumer: pop retries rise from 9.6 to 274 per 1000
   because the slower producer leaves the ring empty more often.

3. Four producers cost far more than the lock.
   At four producers the wall cost is about 126 ns per message (7.94 Mm/s),
   against 25 ns for one producer with the lock. The lock's own cost accounts
   for roughly 15 ns of that; the remaining ~100 ns is contention. The data
   fit cross-core handoffs of the mutex and producers waiting on each other,
   but this run did not isolate them.

4. Four producers spend time parked.
   68 context switches per 1000 messages at four producers, against 0 at one,
   show that losing producers sleep on the futex. Parking adds wake-up latency
   to the claim path. Pop retries at four producers are 1585 per 1000, so the
   consumer frequently finds the oldest slot reserved but unsubmitted and
   waits.

5. Beyond four producers the throughput plateaus.
   From 8 to 24 producers the rate stays at 5.7-6.2 Mm/s while cpu/wall rises
   from 7.8 to 16.7. The extra CPU is spent spinning or parked, not delivering.
   The plateau starts at 8 producers, well before the 20 online CPUs are used
   up, so oversubscription does not cause the drop from 4 to 8.

6. Overwrite mode matches lossless except at one producer.
   At four or more producers nothing is dropped, and the throughput matches
   the lossless table within run-to-run noise. At one producer the consumer
   cannot keep up with the producer and 21.56% of messages are evicted.

Caveats
-------

- Single sweep. Each cell is the median of five runs of one sweep. Between
  this sweep and the previous one, the 4-producer lossless figure moved from
  7.34 to 7.94 Mm/s, so differences under about 10% are not established.
- The "1+lock" row is the only measurement of lock cost; it is one producer
  and so does not show contended handoffs directly.
- Latencies per message are derived from throughput (1 / rate), not measured
  per operation.
- Cross-core and futex explanations in analysis 3 and 4 are inferences from
  the counters. A 2-producer row and a spinlock variant would test them.
